## 1. What the case actually is

It is **not** a sector-ranking exercise. The practical problem:

> Which specific investment opportunities should Kazakhstan actively promote to strategic foreign investors, and why should those investors choose a specific location in Kazakhstan over the strongest realistic alternative?

Two perspectives, in this order:
1. **The strategic investor:** does it make money? Why this location? Why now, or why is it still open?
2. **The investment promotion agency (the client):** which opportunities deserve promotion effort, and what information, coordination or state instrument would convert the opportunity into actual FDI?

Investor economics come first. Agency actionability matters only once the investor case works.

## 2. Starting question

> **Which investor has to put capital somewhere, what actually decides where it goes, can a specific place in Kazakhstan beat the strongest realistic rival on that deciding factor for a sufficiently large reachable market, and why is the opportunity still open?**

## 3. Unit of analysis

Not a sector. An **investor location decision**:

activity / product × target buyers / market × specific place in Kazakhstan × investor type × strongest rival location.

## 4. Hypothesis format

A serious hypothesis must eventually read:

> [Investor type] must [build / expand / enter / diversify / restructure] [activity] for [buyers / reachable market] because [demand / trigger / problem]; the location decision is driven by [deciding factor]; the strongest rival is [X]; Kazakhstan may win because [specific place + specific fact]; the opportunity remains open because [recent change / information gap / coordination problem / other evidenced blocker].

During early generation the Kazakhstan slot may be "?". An incomplete idea cannot stay a serious longlist candidate indefinitely.

## 5. Role of Kazakhstan facts

Generic endowments are **not** a thesis: "Kazakhstan has uranium / copper", "between Europe and Asia", "Kazakhstan imports this product".

Kazakhstan information has three valid roles:
- **A. Origin (specific under-used assets only):** an existing plant, spare capacity, a by-product stream, an existing operator, rare know-how, an under-used infrastructure asset, a specific cost advantage.
- **B. Early feasibility / kill tests:** water, power, gas, logistics, regulation, technology.
- **C. Deep validation:** Kazakhstan vs the strongest rival, in numbers, on the factor that decides the investment.

## 6. Neighbours: three roles

Test each relevant neighbour as:
- **Buyer / reachable market** (real access: duties, freight, time, local competitors);
- **Rival** competing for the same FDI;
- **Trigger** whose constraints or policy changes may reopen an investment decision.

"Access to Central Asia" alone is not evidence.

## 7. The KPMG oral briefing is the operating backbone

Its principles are working tests at every stage, not background:
- investor economics first;
- Kazakhstan-only demand may be too small;
- neighbours are buyers and rivals;
- compare against the strongest rival, not a convenient one;
- water, power, gas and logistics constraints matter;
- technology must be obtainable **and** the operation must stay competitive after transfer;
- strategic fit matters;
- do not simply crowd out existing producers;
- non-obvious and know-how niches differentiate; obvious sectors need a sharp angle;
- every important number needs a working source; assumptions must be explicit;
- the teaser must make economics, market, location and investment size clear from the investor's chair.

Her examples (socks, cables, glass, data centres, critical minerals, etc.) illustrate principles. They are not target sectors.

## 8. Discovery philosophy

**Broad in hypotheses, narrow in expensive research.** Do not explore the whole economy before forming hypotheses.

Useful search lines (not a formal framework):
1. Changes that reopen location decisions; constraints in rival locations.
2. Regional demand currently served inefficiently or from far away.
3. Specific under-used Kazakhstan assets.
4. Trade-data sweep as a coverage check.
5. Assets coming to market: brownfield, privatisation, JV situations.
6. Services and business-model shifts.

The briefing's "trends + Kazakhstan's position + neighbours' position" is a synthesis test, not a mandatory formula.

## 9. Cheap screening

Outcomes before any ranking: **PASS / UWT (unresolved, worth testing) / PARK / FAIL.** A weighted score must never average away a fatal weakness.

Order:
1. Who is the investor, and what actual capital decision exists?
2. What makes the investment relevant / open now?
3. What factor actually decides location?
4. Who is the strongest realistic rival? Ideally named independently by a second person; one person argues the rival's case.
5. Kazakhstan vs that rival on the deciding factor, including water, power and gas where relevant. Does Kazakhstan share the rival's constraint?
6. Reachable market / minimum viable scale.
7. Why has it not happened already?
8. Already captured, or room for another investor?
9. Technology obtainable **and** competitive after transfer?
10. How does the investor earn?
11. Which business model / entry structure makes sense?
12. Rough investment size / capex range.
13. Strategic red flags: crowding out local producers; negative strategic fit.
14. The cheapest fact that could kill the thesis.

**FAIL requires evidence against the idea.** If evidence is missing rather than negative, mark UWT with the exact test, an owner and a time-box. Every kill keeps a one-line reason and source: the kill log is the longlist-to-shortlist logic shown to KPMG.

## 10. Evidence discipline

- Log sources from the first minute; important claims need working links (`opportunities/RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md`).
- Capital-movement evidence: announced / MoU = weak; signed = stronger; financed / under construction / operating = strongest.
- Every decisive assumption is tested over a range, including the value at which the recommendation flips.
- No slide number, rival comparison, project status, policy claim or final decision rests on one AI/model output alone.

## 11. Strategic agenda

Validation, not the source of attractiveness. Translate fit into concrete investor-relevant instruments: tax/customs preferences, land, infrastructure, grid connection, state partner, co-investment, offtake, approval support. Fit can be strong, weak or negative. **Positive fit never rescues weak economics. Negative fit can kill an idea.**

## 12. Business model

Part of the opportunity, not an appendix. Possible structures: greenfield, brownfield, JV, supplier localisation, build-own-operate, tolling, contracted service, acquisition, offtake-backed investment. The return mechanism and rough investment size must be understood before a final niche is selected.

## 13. Winner-level habits (from `BENCHMARK_LESSONS.md`)

- Identify the mechanism that actually decides the outcome.
- Use existing assets, operators and precedents as proof of feasibility.
- Connect several ordinary facts from different sources into a non-obvious insight.
- Reject alternatives visibly, with reasons.
- Treat business model and partnership structure as part of the recommendation.
- Evidence the decisive assumption; keep arithmetic clean.

Two extra tests for finalists:
- **Constraint as lever:** can a Kazakhstan disadvantage (small market, landlocked, climate, etc.) be irrelevant or even beneficial for this specific business?
- **"If it disappeared":** if the clever element were removed, would the recommendation change? If not, it is decoration.

## 14. Status of the original 39-card pass

`opportunities/discovery/independent_pass_claude_2026-10-21.*` is kept, **not deleted**, but it is **not the primary opportunity universe**. It came from a method that over-weighted Kazakhstan-side capabilities. It is useful as:
- **A. a Kazakhstan fact bank:** plants, assets, operators, know-how, material flows, infrastructure, projects, costs, rivals, constraints (re-check sources before use);
- **B. one specific-assets discovery pass.**

No grandfathering: an old hypothesis must satisfy the same investor-location logic (§4, §9) as any new one before it becomes a serious candidate. No extra quarantine machinery.

## 15. How we got here (history, stated accurately)

- The 39 cards did **not** create the original bias. The written method (v2, 20 Oct) already leaned supply-side: Kazakhstan capability was an early/equal idea source, hypotheses were framed around Kazakhstan's advantage, and the rival entered too late (finalist stage).
- The 39-card pass exposed and amplified that bias.
- A later clean-room review, using only the official case, the briefing and `BENCHMARK_LESSONS.md`, independently found essentially the same weak points. It was a robustness check, not a perfectly blind experiment (`BENCHMARK_LESSONS.md` was revised on 21 Oct after the 39-card pass).
- The final method is triangulated by the official case, the oral briefing, the benchmark lessons, the clean-room review and a reconciliation against the previous operating plan. That is enough to **stop methodology redesign** unless research exposes a contradiction.

## 16. Human judgment (not hard-coded)

The team owns: the strategic-investor scale threshold; whether any score is useful after the gates; the final rival choice; interpretation of "why still open"; Top-5 selection and composition; teaser selection; the final narrative. No quotas of "obvious" vs "non-obvious" niches.
