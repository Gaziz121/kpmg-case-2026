# Analysis Playbook

**Status:** PROPOSAL (20 Oct). The team has not adopted it yet.
**For:** any teammate or AI tool analysing an opportunity at any stage. Model-agnostic.
**Companions:** `SCREENING_APPROACH.md` (the funnel) · `RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md` (evidence rules).
Briefing references like "§8" point to `private/kpmg_briefing_notes.md`.

This is not a library of frameworks. It is a set of **habits**: questions to ask every time, traps to catch, and a standard for what good reasoning looks like.

**Default stance:** act as a sceptical investor checking a pitch, not as the opportunity's advocate.

---

## 1. The core loop: run it every time

Before, during and after any piece of analysis, ask:

1. **What decision are we informing?** (A gate? A finalist choice? A teaser number?) If the answer doesn't affect a decision, stop.
2. **What must be true** for this opportunity to work? List 3–6 conditions.
3. **What evidence supports each condition?** Cite it.
4. **What evidence could kill it?** Go and look for that evidence specifically.
5. **What is the weakest assumption?** Name it in every memo, every time.
6. **What are we ignoring?** An alternative opportunity, an alternative explanation, or a reason nobody has done this yet.

Short version for the top of every note: **Decision · Must be true · Evidence for · Evidence that could kill · Weakest assumption.**

---

## 2. Label every statement

Every substantive statement is one of five types. Mark it as such in notes, and make sure slides don't blur them.

| Type | Definition | How it should read |
|---|---|---|
| **Fact** | Stated in a cited source | "Imports were X in 2024 (Source)." |
| **Calculation** | Derived from logged facts, method reproducible | "≈X, calculated as A × B (see Excel tab)." |
| **Assumption** | A stated value we can't source directly | "We assume X (basis: …; range …)." |
| **Inference** | A reasoned conclusion from evidence | "This suggests…, because…" |
| **Judgment** | The team's view where evidence runs out | "We believe…, on balance, because…" |

**Warning signs:** an assumption written as a fact; an inference with no "because"; a judgment presented as data; a number with no source, calculation or assumption ID.

## 3. The Kazakhstan questions

**Why Kazakhstan?** The answer must be *specific* and *checkable*: a named resource, cost, access, capability or policy advantage, tied to this opportunity's main cost or revenue driver.

Not acceptable on their own:
- "Strategic location between Europe and Asia" (§2)
- "Kazakhstan imports a lot of it"
- "It fits the national strategy"
- "Kazakhstan has the raw material" (without showing it is competitive at the next processing step)

**Why Kazakhstan instead of the strongest alternative location?** (Rigour rises by stage: a hypothesis at the gates, a full comparison at the finalist stage.)
- **Pick the right competitor:** where incumbents produce today, where new capacity is being built, or the neighbour with the most similar endowment. Not a weak competitor chosen to make Kazakhstan look good.
- **Compare on the 2–4 drivers that decide the economics** for this opportunity type (see `SCREENING_APPROACH.md` §3.3), not on a generic list.
- **Ask whether the advantage is durable:** will it survive competitor responses, policy changes, the end of a subsidy?
- **If Kazakhstan only matches the competitor,** what extra reason (market access, timing, policy, proximity to the buyer) tips the decision?

---

## 4. The investor questions

**Who actually pays?** Separate the **payer** (who sends money), the **user** and the **beneficiary**. A project where the main beneficiary is "the economy" but no one pays the investor is not investable. For infrastructure and services, identify the contract counterparty and its credit strength.

**Can it reach strategic-investor scale?** Compare the reachable market (domestic + realistic exports, after logistics and tariffs) with the minimum size at which the business makes sense. If the domestic market alone is too small, which export market closes the gap, and can Kazakhstan realistically serve it (§9)?

**What drives investor returns?** Name the 2–3 variables that would move returns most for this opportunity type (price, utilisation, input cost, tariff, take rate, resource grade…). Concentrate the analysis on those. Ignore variables that don't move the answer.

**Why would *this* investor choose it?** What does a strategic investor gain that it can't get elsewhere: market access, cost position, resource access, a platform for the region?

## 5. Trap detectors

Stop and check whenever you notice one of these patterns.

| Trap | The faulty logic | The real question |
|---|---|---|
| **Import substitution** | "Kazakhstan imports it, so producing it locally is an opportunity" | Can a local producer beat the import on delivered cost and quality at a viable scale? (§12) |
| **Market growth ≠ share** | "The global market is growing fast" | What share can a Kazakhstan-based producer realistically win, and why? |
| **National benefit ≠ return** | "This creates jobs and diversification" | Does the investor earn an adequate return without that argument? (§8) |
| **Resources ≠ advantage** | "Kazakhstan has the resource" | Is it competitive on grade, cost and logistics at the step being proposed? |
| **Demand + inputs ≠ advantage** | "There's demand and we have the inputs" | Why not produce it in the competitor location that also has both? (§10) |
| **Policy ≠ demand** | "The government plans to support this sector" | Is there a paying customer independent of the plan? |
| **Generic geography** | "Kazakhstan connects East and West" | What specific cost or time saving, for which product, on which route? |
| **Historical-data bias** | "Trade data shows no activity, so there's no opportunity" | Is there a structural change (technology, policy, rerouting, prices) that makes history a poor guide? |
| **Trend hype** (the opposite trap) | "This is the industry of the future" | What evidence shows the trend reaches Kazakhstan, and can Kazakhstan compete in it? (§11) |
| **Displacement** | "Foreign investment will replace local producers" | Does the investment add value rather than just displace domestic firms? (§18) |

---

## 6. Structural trends vs historical data

Historical statistics describe what has happened. They miss opportunities created by **change**. Ask:

- **What has changed recently or is about to change** (technology, trade regime, logistics, energy prices, regulation, a competitor's constraint)?
- **What is the causal chain** from that change to a paying customer for a Kazakhstan-based business?
- **Is there an analogue:** a country or market where a similar change already produced investment?
- **What leading indicators exist** (announced projects, price signals, policy texts, investor statements)?
- **What would prove the trend thesis wrong,** and how soon would we know?

A trend-based idea is defensible when the chain, the analogue and the leading indicators are explicit, even if historical data is thin. Use UWT, not FAIL, while it is being tested.

---

## 7. What are we ignoring?

- **"If this is so attractive, why hasn't it been done?"** Possible answers: something has recently changed; there is an information gap; there is a barrier that can be fixed; or it isn't actually attractive. Know which applies.
- **Alternative opportunities:** is there an adjacent opportunity (a step up or down the value chain, a different target market, a different granularity) with a stronger case?
- **Alternative explanations:** could the data pattern we see (rising imports, a price spread) have a different cause than the one we assume?
- **Who loses** if this happens, and could they block it?

## 8. The sceptical investment committee

Run this as a challenge round (V4) before an idea becomes a finalist. The person challenging did not build the case and argues to reject it.

1. Who exactly is the customer, and why would they switch to you?
2. Why here and not in [strongest alternative location]? Show me the numbers.
3. What do I need to believe about price and cost for this to pay back?
4. What's your weakest assumption, and what happens if it's 30% wrong?
5. How big is the market *you* can reach, not the global market?
6. What stops incumbents from cutting prices the day we start production?
7. Who provides the technology, and why would they share it?
8. What could go wrong that would be very hard to reverse (water, power, logistics, regulation, sanctions)?
9. How long until first revenue, and what's the capital needed until then?
10. Is this investable without government support? If not, how reliable is that support?
11. What evidence would convince you this is wrong, and have you looked for it?
12. Why is this better than the opportunity you didn't choose?

Record the challenges that weren't answered convincingly. They become either further tests, stated risks with mitigation, or reasons to drop the idea.

---

## 9. Standards for output

- **Answer first, then support it.** State the conclusion, then the evidence.
- **Name the uncertainty.** State confidence and the weakest assumption.
- **Precision matches the evidence.** Use ranges and round numbers; no false decimals.
- **Write specifically.** No generic consulting phrases ("unlocking potential", "strategic hub", "synergies") without the concrete claim behind them. KPMG warned that obviously AI-generated work may be skipped (§22).
- **Cite every number** with an evidence ID.
- **Be consistent across the five analyses:** same terms, same structure, same source conventions (§23).

---

## 10. Stage checklists

**Idea card (Discovery):** idea · lens · why it might matter. Nothing else.

**Longlist row:** draft thesis sentence · Kazakhstan advantage hypothesis · payers · target markets · main failure risk · ≥1 evidence lead.

**Gate pass:** every gate PASS / FAIL / UWT / PARK; a FAIL cites evidence against; a UWT names its test and owner; red flags noted.

**Shortlist note (≤1 page):** research brief (§1 of Research Protocol) · Q1–Q6 short answers with evidence IDs · confidence · weakest assumption · traps checked (§5).

**Finalist memo:** V1 Kazakhstan vs best alternative · V2 economics for the opportunity type · V3 triangulated market · V4 challenge-round record · V5 all UWTs closed · risks and mitigations.

---

## 11. Instructions for AI tools using this playbook

- Follow the core loop (§1) and label statement types (§2) in every output.
- Don't invent numbers, sources or URLs. If no source is found, say so and propose an assumption with its basis.
- Actively generate the strongest counter-argument to any opportunity you analyse.
- Flag any trap from §5 that the analysis is at risk of.
- Leave selection, kill decisions on non-obvious ideas, and final wording to the human team.
- Don't start substantive opportunity research unless the team has explicitly approved it.
