**id:** SG-R3

**opportunity_name:** Certified intermediate-crop (camelina-type) oil supply and crushing hub in Kazakhstan's northern grain belt, for EU-compliant sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) made from fats and oils (HEFA SAF).

**investor_type:**
- FACT: an industry coalition lobbying on the EU's list of advanced biofuel feedstocks ("Annex IX") includes bp, Chevron, Moeve, Neste, Louis Dreyfus, Repsol and SkyNRG. The coalition's report was paid for by Bayer, Bunge, Camelina Company, Corteva and Nufarm.
- FACT: Chevron and Bunge already own an Argentine camelina/carinata seed business (2023).
- FACT: Hong Kong's EcoCeres, Full Vision Capital and Towngas are exploring an oilseed-based SAF plant in Kazakhstan.
- INFERENCE: a likely structure is a JV with a Kazakh agri-holding or crusher.

**capital_decision:**
- INFERENCE: where to build the first non-EU origin for "intermediate crop" feedstock that counts as advanced under EU rules. That means seed supply, contract farming, certification, and a crushing/pretreatment plant.
- ASSUMPTION: output of 50–100 kt of oil a year.
- UNKNOWN: capex (not established).
- ASSUMPTION: oil yield of 0.15–0.35 t per hectare.
- INFERENCE (calculation from the two assumptions above): 100 kt of oil needs about 0.29–0.67 Mha. That is roughly 8–20% of Kazakhstan's 2025 oilseed area (see kz_location_asset).

**buyer_reachable_market:**
- FACT (EU aviation regulator EASA): in 2025, 85% of EU SAF feedstock was imported, 61% from China, and 80% was used cooking oil.
- FACT (EASA): about 2.1 Mt a year of bio-SAF is needed for the 2030 6% minimum.
- FACT (SkyNRG): EU SAF demand is 3 Mt in 2030 and 10.3 Mt in 2035. Europe's HEFA and co-processing capacity (3.9 Mt by 2035) is larger than its domestic feedstock (~2 Mt a year).
- FACT (SkyNRG): Indonesia and Malaysia are restricting exports of used cooking oil and palm-mill effluent.
- INFERENCE: there is also an eastern exit — selling oil by rail to Asian HEFA producers that export SAF to the EU.

**trigger_why_now:**
- FACT: a 2024 EU directive (2024/1405) added intermediate crops to the advanced list ("Part A") for aviation. Condition: they must come from areas where a short vegetation period limits food/feed crops to one harvest. They also must not create demand for extra land, and soil organic matter must be kept.
- FACT: the EU SAF mandate (ReFuelEU, Art. 4(4)) caps biofuels that are neither advanced nor waste-oil-type at 3%. Art. 4(5) excludes food and feed crops.
- INFERENCE: so the origin's qualification status directly sets the value of the oil.
- FACT: in 2025–26 the EU put prohibitive duties on Russian agricultural goods (flax: 20%, then 50%).

**kz_location_asset:**
- FACT: Kazakhstan's oilseed area was 3.4 Mha in 2025 (+0.5 Mha): Kostanay 755 kha, North Kazakhstan 760 kha, Akmola 430 kha. The target is 4 Mha by 2029.
- FACT: an EU-bound oilseed chain already exists. Kazakh flax was 72.5% of EU flax imports in July 2025–June 2026 (497.8 kt).
- FACT: there is a certification precedent. BioOperations' plant in Tayinsha shipped bioethanol certified under ISCC (the main EU biofuel certification scheme) to Belgium.
- FACT: policy pushes crushing inside the country: a 20% export duty on sunflower seed and subsidies for building processing plants.
- INFERENCE: the north is a single-harvest system.
- UNKNOWN: fallow area.

**strongest_alternative:**
- INFERENCE: under the current rules, EU domestic intermediate crops.
- FACT: TotalEnergies/Avril in France plan 10–30 kt of oil by 2028–30.
- INFERENCE: Camelina Company works in Spain.
- INFERENCE: they are the easiest to certify.
- FACT (T&E): the NGO Transport & Environment (T&E) pushes "local first".
- INFERENCE: if the pending rule revision relaxes the one-harvest condition, Argentina/Brazil.
- FACT (T&E): their potential is more than 10× the EU's.
- FACT: Chevron/Bunge already have a platform there (the Argentine camelina/carinata seed business; see investor_type).

**plausible_kz_deciding_advantage:**
- INFERENCE: the deciding factor is the volume of certifiable advanced-category oil at a competitive delivered cost.
- INFERENCE: Kazakhstan meets the single-harvest condition, which multi-harvest South America may fail.
- INFERENCE: it has a large contiguous grain belt where oilseed rotation is already growing.
- FACT: it already exports oilseeds to the EU (see kz_location_asset).
- INFERENCE: the EU's Russian tariffs don't apply to it.

**why_still_open:**
- FACT: the EU certification rules (a revision of implementing regulation 2022/996) were still pending as of May 2026.
- FACT: the EcoCeres plan was only "under consideration" as of August 2026.
- FACT: KazMunayGas's SAF projects use the alcohol-to-jet route, made from bioethanol.
- INFERENCE: grain-based ethanol is excluded from the EU SAF minimum shares, so those projects don't capture this niche.
- FACT (industry-funded report): demand for advanced-category aviation feedstock stays weak until about 2035.
- UNKNOWN: whether a Kazakh camelina-for-EU programme already exists. None was found.

**cheapest_falsification_test:**
- Qualification test. Get the draft revision of the EU certification rules, or ask ISCC/2BSvs (the main certification schemes) in writing: does camelina sown on fallow land in a north-Kazakh wheat–fallow rotation qualify as advanced under the new aviation entry? - Economics test. Ask one HEFA buyer (EcoCeres first, given its live interest) for the indicative 2028–32 price premium of certified advanced-category oil over used cooking oil. If the premium doesn't cover rail freight, certification and the yield penalty on the next wheat crop → kill.
