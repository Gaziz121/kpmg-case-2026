### BK-002 card — Soviet-design turbine/boiler repair-and-parts centre (route 1)

**External fact → problem → capital decision → Kazakhstan hypothesis.**
Kazakhstan's 2026 programme puts 51 turbines and 55 boilers into capital repair (FACT, MinEnergo via inbusiness 10 Mar 2026). Plant owners are spending 384bn tenge on repairs this year, and five CHPs remain in the "red zone" (FACT, Kapital 27 Jul 2026). The fleet is Soviet-design. Deputies stated that Kazakhstan has only repair capacity, not turbine production, and that traditional Russian and Ukrainian suppliers can no longer guarantee deliveries; missing equipment has already disrupted heating-season preparation (FACT, inbusiness 2 Nov 2023). The supply base is visibly shifting: Russian turbines for Ekibastuz GRES-2 were dropped for Harbin, and Astana TPP-2 casing parts are to be built in China to Leningrad Metal Plant (LMZ) drawings (FACT, 2026). The capital decision: a non-Russian OEM or service firm builds an in-country repair and parts shop under multi-year agreements with fragmented plant owners. The Kazakhstan logic (INFERENCE): repairs must fit the short summer window before the heating season, so an in-country shop beats shipping rotors abroad. Agency role (JUDGMENT): potentially strong, by aggregating fragmented owner demand into framework service contracts.

**Falsification note — BK-002**
- Objection tested: a domestic or foreign incumbent already does this well, so there is no friction left.
- Found: no foreign steam-turbine service shop was found in KZ, and the OEM moves found are new-build MoUs. However, who performs repairs today is not established, and the friction evidence is mostly from 2022–23.
- Decisive remaining uncertainty: the current repair contractors and parts sources, and whether demand outside Kazakhstan (Uzbekistan etc.) is reachable enough to give a foreign investor scale.

### BK-003 card — ShalkiyaZinc stake sale (route 2)

**External fact → problem → capital decision → Kazakhstan hypothesis.**
Tau-Ken Samruk is privatizing ShalkiyaZinc, the developer of the Shalkiya lead-zinc deposit in Kyzylorda region. It wants a strategic investor to finance and complete the processing plant (planned 4 Mt/yr of ore) and the remaining infrastructure. EY has been doing the valuation and investor sounding since summer 2025, and Tau-Ken would keep a "substantial" stake (FACT, inbusiness 30 Oct 2025). The project was costed at about $691m, partly financed by a $175m EBRD loan with a Samruk-Kazyna guarantee (FACT, inbusiness 12 Jun 2024). As of 1 Jan 2026 Tau-Ken was still the sole holder of 5% or more (FACT, KASE). The capital decision: a miner or smelter buys into a deposit where construction has started, with ore already prepared, rather than starting greenfield. The process can close: Tau-Ken sold 70% of the Severny Katpar tungsten deposit to a US buyer for $114.8m in February 2026 (FACT, Vlast). Main risk (FACT): low ore grade was a known problem in Soviet times, and sale talk has dragged since 2021–2024. Agency role (JUDGMENT): moderate, in widening the bidder pool beyond domestic and Kazzinc-linked buyers.

**Falsification note — BK-003**
- Objection tested: repeated failure to sell shows the asset is uneconomic (low grade) or already earmarked for a domestic buyer.
- Found: there was no sale by Jan 2026 and the low-grade history is real. However, the EY process is formal and live (2025), and Tau-Ken closed a comparable foreign stake sale in 2026. Earmarking for Kazzinc remains an unconfirmed rumour.
- Decisive remaining uncertainty: grade and recovery economics from the latest reserve recalculation, and the stake and price Tau-Ken expects.

### BK-004 card — Butadiene LLP equity slot after Tatneft's exit (route 2)

**External fact → problem → capital decision → Kazakhstan hypothesis.**
Butadiene LLP is building a complex of about $1.1bn in Atyrau region to turn Tengiz butane into butadiene, synthetic rubbers and MTBE. Lummus licenses the technology, Sinopec Guangzhou holds the EPC contract, and construction began in May 2026 (FACT, Kapital; Forbes.kz; nationalbusiness). The UK sanctioned Tatneft on 18 Dec 2025, and Tatneft exited its 75% in January 2026. Experts cite the sanctions and the risk to Western technology licences, not project economics (FACT, Forbes.kz; nationalbusiness 6 Feb 2026). Samruk-Kazyna now holds 99.999% and bridges the capex with yuan bonds bought by the parent itself, and long-term financing is "not guaranteed" (FACT, nationalbusiness 27 Jul 2026). The problem: a state-held project with no industrial partner and no external financier. The capital decision: a rubber or petrochemical strategic takes equity and brings offtake and financing. The Kazakhstan logic: formula-priced Tengiz butane at about $90/t versus about $150/t at market (analyst estimate, Forbes.kz), plus a licence and EPC already in place. Agency role (JUDGMENT): strong, because no partner process is visible and the state is financing alone.

**Falsification note — BK-004**
- Objection tested: Tatneft left because the economics are bad, or the state intends to keep 100%, so there is no investable slot.
- Found: the exit is attributed to UK sanctions and licence risk, not economics. However, no partner search has been announced, and the planned rubber buyer (Tengri Tyres) is itself Tatneft-linked.
- Decisive remaining uncertainty: whether Samruk would sell equity, and whether rubber offtake and spreads hold against Chinese capacity.
