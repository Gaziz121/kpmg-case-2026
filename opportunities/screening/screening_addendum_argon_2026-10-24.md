### OP-102 — Organophosphorus intermediates and derivatives in Taraz — UWT

**Decision / hypothesis.** A specialty phosphorus producer or qualified technology/equity partner considers a Taraz derivative plant, integrating local P4 with chlorine to serve EU formulators and regional industrial users. The named companies are prospective investors, not evidenced bidders. The card's USD 80–150m CAPEX and output ranges remain **ASSUMPTIONS**, not estimates validated here.

**Must be true.** Usable P4 and chlorine must be contractable at a competitive combined cost; product-specific reachable demand must load the investment; the site must have utilities and lawful logistics; and real local manufacture must have defensible EU treatment and an available investor slot.

**Cheapest kill / evidence.**

- **FACT:** DBK currently lists Sastobe as under financing, with caustic/PVC/calcium-carbide scope [S01]. **FACT:** a Kazakh Invest proposal hosted by government explicitly lists **12.1 kt/year liquid chlorine**, but its document date is **August 2021**, not the crawler's apparent recent date [S02, p.1]. **INFERENCE:** neither “all chlorine is captive” nor “merchant chlorine is now available” is established. The old sale-product specification warrants a current surplus/offtake check; DBK financing proves neither commissioning nor an allocation to Taraz.
- **CALCULATION, ideal material balance only:** chlorine is about 77.4% of PCl3 mass. If the card's 30–50 kt/year chlorination output were all PCl3, it would require approximately **23–39 kt/year chlorine** and **6.8–11.3 kt/year P4**, before losses. Thus 12.1 kt of old planned chlorine output would not alone cover that configuration. Product mix changes the requirement; this is a sizing check, not a process design or proof of infeasibility. Chlor-alkali co-production is approximately **1.13 t dry NaOH per t chlorine**, so a dedicated supply route also needs a credible caustic outlet. A caustic price/volume credit is not free feedstock.
- **FACT:** the NDFZ operator's indexed site advertises yellow-phosphorus production capacity [S03]. **UNKNOWN:** current unencumbered tonnes, transfer price, utility cost, contracting entity and JV/site rights. Do not equate historical Kazphosphate branding, capacity or phosphorus reserves with a captive supply entitlement.
- **FACT:** the definitive EU measure confirms the PBTC duty trigger and rejects the claimed inadequacy of existing Union capacity [S05]. The provisional measure was provisional, resolving the source card's wording conflict [S06]. **INFERENCE:** an anti-dumping duty is neither an importer margin nor proof of a physical supply shortage. PBTC demand cannot underwrite the whole multi-product plant without separate buyer/product allocation.

**Buyer / scale.** EU formulators and regional scale-inhibitor purchasers are credible buyer classes. The EU investigation reports PBTC consumption of **11.3–15.0 kt** in its investigation period [S06, Table 2, recital 193]; that is not an unserved market or an order for the card's wider derivative capacity. HEDP, other phosphonates, DMPI and phosphate esters require their own specifications and customers. The PBTC measure does not apply indiscriminately to every organophosphorus product. Regional reachable volumes remain UNKNOWN.

**Strongest alternative / return.** **INFERENCE:** benchmark the Vietnamese DGC chain, including P4 at Lao Cai and its Nghi Son chemical investment, plus EU conversion using imported P4. DGC's own release establishes construction commencement, not present operation [S04]. It does not establish same-site integration of every step. The card's Vietnamese power/ore cost squeeze and “zero logistical reach” claim are not adopted. Taraz could earn a conversion margin and avoid some hazardous upstream transport, but only if full feedstock, co-reactant, utilities, freight and capital costs beat those alternatives. No competitive power tariff or firm power, water or gas allocation was obtained. Classify each derivative and its route separately; the card's IBC-container code is not the chemical's tariff code, and stable packaging does not establish non-hazardous cargo status.

**Capture / why open.** No binding award closing the entire derivative investment was located in the targeted source checks. This is **UNKNOWN**, not proof of an open foreign JV. The trade-defense change is real; coordinated feedstock supply and the opening's accessibility remain unproved. Sastobe financing does not confer its chlorine or equity rights on another investor.

**Legal hinge.** **FACT:** Article 13 permits extension of duties where its circumvention conditions are met; customs origin uses a separate substantive-processing/economic-justification test [S07–S08]. **INFERENCE:** Chinese ownership is not automatically disqualifying, and Kazakh incorporation/local inputs do not guarantee immunity. A local-feedstock cost rationale independent of duty avoidance must be documented. Do not extrapolate assembly thresholds into a chemical-manufacturing safe harbour. Duty durability over commissioning and repayment is UNKNOWN.

**Strongest NO case.** CFO: a chlorine plant and unsold caustic could consume the derivative margin. Rival: DGC already funds its own chemical chain; Kazakhstan's cheaper combined inputs are unproved. Judge: a product-specific trade measure and a historical chlorine plan cannot demonstrate current investment access. The evidence above does not close those objections; they determine UWT.

**Exact next tests / support / weaken-or-kill conditions.**

1. **First, 45-minute supply-document test:** obtain the current Sastobe commissioning schedule, net chlorine balance after PVC/other uses, committed sales and a Taraz delivered quote; obtain NDFZ's available P4 allocation and site/JV terms. Reconcile the actual product mix. **Support:** both inputs are allocable on a mutually compatible timetable, with a costed logistics route. **Weaken:** the old chlorine sale stream has disappeared or insufficient residual volume remains. **Kill only if evidenced:** no feasible competitive supplementary chlorine route exists, or required P4/site access is definitively unavailable.
2. **Only if supply survives, 60-minute cost-sheet test:** compare Taraz with Vietnamese and EU alternatives on the same accepted product basket, including caustic realization if dedicated electrolysis is needed, utility connection/firmness and co-reactants. **Support:** a plausible margin and capital recovery survive adverse input and selling-price cases without treating the duty as profit. **PARK:** only a subsidy or speculative caustic sale makes it work; **FAIL:** quotes establish a structural inability to compete for the actual product/market.
3. **Trade reviewer, 45-minute legal-document screen:** map actual products, feedstock origins, transformation, independent economic rationale and implementation timetable against customs origin and Article 13. **Support:** a defensible route with commercial value beyond simple duty avoidance. **Weaken/kill:** counsel establishes an applicable trade barrier that eliminates the required margin. No guarantee against investigation is requested or implied.

**Weakest assumption:** economically available chlorine and P4 can be assembled into one competitive project. **JUDGMENT:** UWT, not FAIL on missing supply data and not PASS on tariff arithmetic alone.

### OP-103 — OEM automotive safety-glazing fabrication — UWT

**Decision / hypothesis.** A Tier-1 fabricator considers bending, lamination, tempering, printing and encapsulation near Almaty or Kostanay customers. Kyzylorda is a substrate/site option, not an established lowest-cost choice. The card's **200–250 thousand vehicle sets/year** is an **ASSUMPTION** about capacity, not an evidenced minimum efficient scale. No CAPEX is invented.

**Must be true.** Sufficient model-level programmes must accept glass deletion from CKD kits, remain unawarded and cover tooling/qualification costs; suitable substrate must be available; and delivered approved sets must compete with qualified imports.

**Cheapest kill / buyer scale.** **FACT:** KAU reports **102,870 passenger vehicles** produced in January–July 2026, versus 111,115 vehicles/other equipment overall, with materially different output across four main plants [S09]. **CALCULATION:** 102,870 × 12/7 ≈ **176,000 passenger vehicles/year**, a simple run-rate illustration, not a forecast. It corroborates the government's **177,000 passenger-car plan**, which is also a plan, not realized CKD demand [S10]. Do not count trailers, SKD vehicles with glass already installed, exports unavailable to the supplier, or announced nameplate as glass-set nominations. Brand totals are not model/SKU totals.

**INFERENCE:** even the entire illustrated passenger run rate would fill only about **70–88%** of the assumed line nameplate; the accessible share is lower unless ramp-up, exports or aftermarket demand add volume. This does not prove a smaller/modular line uneconomic. It makes awarded models, toolkit amortization and break-even loading decisive. Aftermarket demand is not independently sized and is not used to close the gap.

**Substrate / physical constraints.** **FACT:** Orda's own current product page advertises **3–12 mm** float and broad automotive uses [S11]. This differs from the card's narrower architectural-range wording, but does not certify **1.8–2.1 mm**, optical quality, automotive campaign economics or OEM acceptance. **UNKNOWN:** suitable thin substrate and delivered price. Imported flat substrate is a cost scenario to quote, not an automatic kill; proximity savings could survive it. Site power/gas reliability and connection costs, qualified workforce, processing yields and working capital remain unpriced. UN R43 confirms a safety-glazing approval regime [S14]; generic float manufacture is not approval of curved glazing, and regulatory approval alone is not an OEM nomination or IATF qualification.

**Capture / why now.** **FACT:** government identifies operating assembly additions and an active component-localization agenda [S10]. The Kyzylorda city report lists Komfort's **KZT 5.5bn tempered-glass expansion**; regional authorities describe Orda's **USD 55m planned expansion** [S12–S13]. Neither published description establishes OEM curved/laminated-glass nominations or excludes automotive scope. Do not call these plans commissioned capacity. **UNKNOWN:** a Tier-1 entry slot and binding kit-supplier commitments. An omission of glazing from a public component list is not evidence of an unawarded programme.

**Strongest alternative / mechanism.** Benchmark **continued qualified China/Korea kit supply first**, then Uzbekistan's Avtooyna, and eligible Russian suppliers. UzAuto's own history establishes actual automotive-set production at Avtooyna [S15]; it does not establish spare export capacity or qualification for each Kazakh model. **INFERENCE:** local fabrication could reduce curved-glass rack freight, damage, inventory and delivery risk. **UNKNOWN:** the savings versus model-specific tooling, changeovers, imported substrate, OEM transfer pricing and deletion penalties. Russian supply is not assumed universally prohibited; eligibility is buyer-specific. Uzbekistan's non-EAEU status does not alone establish a particular duty or localization-value gap. Localization support is a potential commercial benefit only after actual assembly-agreement terms and OEM credits are read.

**Strongest NO case.** CFO: national output does not amortize each model's tooling, especially if kit deletion earns little credit. Rival: qualified incumbents spread tools and development over larger global/regional programmes. Judge: neither a float furnace nor generic tempered-glass expansion establishes approved thin substrate or a free supplier nomination. No public source closes these central objections.

**Exact next tests / support / weaken-or-kill conditions.**

1. **First, 60-minute procurement-document test:** assemble one actual Almaty or Kostanay model basket with 3–5-year CKD volumes, glass-set BOMs, nomination/end dates, permissible deletion credit and tooling ownership. **Support:** unawarded, deletable programmes support the fabricator/vendor's quoted break-even loading after SKU changeovers. **PARK:** residual volumes are too fragmented or approvals arrive too late. **FAIL only on evidence:** binding nominations exclude the entire target basket or kit terms make local substitution structurally uneconomic.
2. **45-minute scope/specification test:** read Orda and Komfort equipment/product/approval lists and substrate certificates. **Support:** the proposed fabrication capability remains open, with accepted local substrate or a competitively priced imported-sheet route. **Weaken/kill:** a binding incumbent programme captures the same buyer scope, or neither substrate route can satisfy the required cost/specification. “Tempered glass” alone resolves neither condition.
3. **60-minute like-for-like quote test:** compare one model's approved local set with landed China/Korea, eligible Avtooyna and Russian offers; include tooling per nominated lifetime volume, validation, racks/breakage/return freight, stock and actual localization value. Test low/base/high awarded volumes and both substrate routes. **Support:** the local set has a plausible capital-recovering price or a documented buyer-paid service advantage. **PARK/FAIL:** a small timing gap or structural negative economics respectively, established from comparable terms.

**Weakest assumption:** enough OEM-approved, economically deletable model volume is still available to a new supplier. **JUDGMENT:** UWT; demand and the fabrication distinction are credible, but aggregate plant capacity is insufficient evidence for PASS.

### OP-104 — Multi-seed Hi-Pro meal and lecithin processing — UWT

**Decision / hypothesis.** A strategic crusher/feed or ingredient partner considers multi-seed extraction plus deep-dehulling and lecithin upgrading for EU meal/ingredient buyers and Chinese/Central Asian oil buyers. Evaluate the **admitted brownfield fallback first**; no new opportunity is invented. The card's USD 60–110m greenfield package, throughput, yields, meal protein, crush spread and lecithin price remain **ASSUMPTIONS**. A lower-cost upgrade has not been priced.

**Must be true.** Contractable crop-specific seed and an accessible plant/partner must exist; higher-value specifications must earn an incremental margin; combined oil/meal/lecithin realizations after logistics must beat destination crushing and existing domestic processors; access approvals must fit the commercial timetable.

**Cheapest kill / existing capacity.** **FACT:** the competition authority reports **4.2m t/year** processing capacity with approximately **47% utilization** for its 2024/H1-2025 sunflower-oil market study [S16]. The May 2026 government plan separately records **50.4% national oilseed-processing utilization in 2025**; regional results differ and later columns are targets [S17]. **INFERENCE:** broad idle capacity is evidenced; these aggregates do not prove more than 30% idle *modern solvent* capacity at every candidate site.

**FACT:** Qazaq-Astyq already advertises **1,000 t/day**, high-protein meal and food/feed phosphatide concentrate [S18]. **INFERENCE:** ordinary extraction, protein meal and a lecithin precursor are not vacant capabilities. Phosphatide concentrate is not automatically standardized/fractionated lecithin of the proposed buyer grade. **UNKNOWN:** the remaining equipment, specification and buyer gap. A new crusher cannot be justified merely by attaching these product names.

**Seed / timing / market.** Official reporting supports established oil and meal export channels and rising installed capacity [S19]. The 13 October harvest update expects **over 5m tonnes of oilseeds** [S20]; that is a forecast for mixed crops, not available contracted feedstock at one plant. **UNKNOWN:** grade, crop, catchment, existing contracts and seasonal working capital. The reported Russian sunflower restriction is a temporary phytosanitary certificate action [S25], not verified durable economic protection. It may tighten seed procurement, rather than create a cheaper feedstock wedge. It is not a linseed/rapeseed restriction.

**Trade mechanism: what survives the check.**

- **FACT:** the cited EU schedule distinguishes Russian/Belarusian goods from ordinary linseed, sunflower and rapeseed meals; the latter lines are free [S21]. **INFERENCE:** this can favor eligible Kazakh-origin supply over Russian-origin supply, but also favors an EU crusher importing eligible Kazakh seed. It does not decide the location of crushing.
- **FACT:** oil duty is product/use-specific: the schedule shows crude linseed oil at **3.2%**, while certain crude rapeseed lines distinguish **3.2%/6.4%**; refined lines differ [S21]. Do not apply a blanket oil percentage or treat meal's classification as lecithin's classification. Current shipment-specific tariff and origin treatment still need checking.
- **FACT:** EUDR's listed soya products cover seed, flour/meal, oil and extraction residues; **lecithin CN 2923 20 is not in that soya list**, including the checked 2026 amendment [S22]. **INFERENCE:** the card's claim that non-soy lecithin replaces EUDR-regulated soy lecithin does not establish a direct regulatory premium. Non-soy meal may benefit from soy-related feed substitution, but Kazakh seed crushed in Europe is also non-soy. FEFAC's disruption estimate is an industry scenario, not Kazakh purchase commitments [S24]. The Commission's current application dates are recorded in S23; the investment cannot assume today's scenario premium survives its own commissioning.

**Strongest alternative / return.** Benchmark destination crushing of **the same Kazakh seed in Belgium/Baltics**, with Ukraine as a product-specific meal/oil competitor, and an existing Kazakh processor's upgrade/tolling offer as the local capital alternative. **INFERENCE:** split destinations may avoid westbound transport of the oil fraction, but higher meal/hull separation, lower meal value density, eastbound freight, seasonal storage and two buyer channels can absorb the saving. Neither EUDR exemption nor Russian tariffs prove a Kazakhstan-versus-Europe crush margin. No route quote establishes Russian transit/port availability or cost. Origin acquisition and proof fall under different customs provisions [S08]; the source card's “Article 60 non-manipulation documentation” is not accepted as a complete customs rule. Segregate actual seed origins and verify each route with the importer/broker.

**Capture / opening / why not already.** Existing processors and export channels evidence partial capture of the obvious business. No plant-specific foreign equity, lease or upgrade mandate was established. **INFERENCE:** missing premium-processing capability/offtake coordination could leave a brownfield opening; neither is yet evidenced. China market access at industry level is credible [S19], but **UNKNOWN** for the proposed facility, oil type and start date. A generic CIFER reference is not approval of all meal/feed and oil streams, nor a verified registration lead time.

**Strongest NO case.** CFO: building commodity capacity beside idle plants ties up capital without additional feedstock. Rival: Europe can crush the same tariff-eligible, non-soy Kazakh seed near its buyers. Judge: advertised domestic meal/concentrate output and the lecithin scope correction leave the claimed incremental premium unproved. The brownfield fallback keeps a bounded test worthwhile; it does not establish an attractive project.

**Exact next tests / support / weaken-or-kill conditions.**

1. **First, 60-minute asset/specification audit:** select one existing northern/eastern site from the admitted geography; obtain actual crop-specific capacity/utilization, seed commitments, current meal/concentrate specifications, upgrade equipment and partner availability. **Support:** an accessible upgrade can produce a paid-for specification absent from the existing output without duplicating unused extraction capacity. **PARK:** only undifferentiated greenfield crushing remains, or no partner/specification gap is identifiable. **FAIL only on evidence:** the necessary feedstock or specified partner access is definitively unavailable and no admitted configuration survives.
2. **60-minute matched netback test, conditional on usable quotes:** for one crop and harvest date compare (a) Kazakh processing, with meal west and oil east/south, (b) exporting that seed for Belgium/Baltic crushing, and (c) an existing Kazakh toll/upgrade offer. Include seed opportunity cost, crop-specific yields/hulls, accepted protein/digestibility basis, certification, both freight legs, tariffs/origin handling, working capital and incremental annualized CAPEX. Do not reuse one generic yield for every crop. Set the speculative EUDR lecithin premium to **zero**. **Support:** a positive capital-recovering incremental margin survives adverse freight, seed and product-price cases. **Weaken/kill:** required freight/premium exceeds buyer bids; PARK marginal economics, FAIL only a demonstrated structural contradiction across the admitted configurations.
3. **Commercial/access reviewer, 45-minute documentary check:** match one EU meal/lecithin specification and one oil buyer's terms with the actual site's export approvals; verify the relevant Chinese facility/product registry and authority requirements if China is essential, or quote the admitted Central Asian outlet instead. **Support:** both outlets can accept the output on a compatible timetable. **Weaken/kill:** a binding access barrier or missing outlet destroys the split-stream netback; no unverified registration duration is assumed.

**Weakest assumption:** an incremental protein/lecithin upgrade earns enough *net* value beyond ordinary crushing to beat both local incumbents and destination processing. **JUDGMENT:** UWT for that admitted testable package. Current evidence gives no greenfield justification; it does not establish that every brownfield upgrade is uneconomic.
