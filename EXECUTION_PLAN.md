Approximately **60–90 minutes total per person** is practical guidance, not a hard clock or a synchronization requirement. Limit scope, not collaboration timing. Keep both routes isolated from prior lists until the owner's own discovery output is frozen; after that the clean-room purpose is finished (§5).

- Up to two usable distinct hypotheses per route; no minimum; at most four per person. A route may return zero; its search-record entries then show what was explored. No separate completion note.
- Roughly 150–200 words per card, excluding links and field names. Show the causal chain: **external fact → investor/customer problem → capital decision → Kazakhstan hypothesis**. A polished narrative without an evidenced external anchor is insufficient.
- Provide initial source links supporting specific claims; prefer company annual reports, investor presentations, capex announcements, procurement plans, operator materials, project disclosures, regulatory/trade data and relevant industry associations. For every submitted card, the human researcher opens and inspects the decisive source anchor(s) and confirms support for the attributed claims. No mandatory source count or citation-count quality score. Do not pad citations or fill missing evidence with AI inference; record the gap as `not established`.
- Label substantive statements FACT / CALCULATION / ASSUMPTION / INFERENCE / JUDGMENT. Unknown fields remain `not established` or `unknown`.
- **Mandatory quality control per submitted card:** (1) the human opens the decisive source; (2) one targeted, cheap attempt is made to weaken or kill the central inference — for example, an opening already captured/unavailable, no real investor/customer problem, a capital decision that is not investable, an obvious rival that dominates, or an unsupported Kazakhstan-specific causal link; (3) unsupported links remain `not established`. A second AI/context is optional, used only when a central inference is consequential or uncertain, under the same clean-room restrictions. Record a three-line falsification note by working ID: **objection tested** (the strongest cheap objection); **found**; **decisive remaining uncertainty**. This is discovery verification, not deep validation.
- No ranking, stars, Top 5, detailed DCF/IRR, long sector reports or promotional language. Do not solve the whole case.
- Stop a route when it has been meaningfully explored, useful candidates have external evidence anchors, obvious contrary explanations have received a targeted check and important gaps are disclosed. Further searches increasingly repeat known actors, documents or mechanisms, and more open-ended searching is unlikely to improve the screening universe enough to justify delaying validation and production. Weak ideas must not fill the cap. Stop pursuing a lead if it remains only a sector/trend, lacks an articulable investor decision or paying market, relies on a generic Kazakhstan advantage, or encounters an evidenced fatal contradiction. Record it as one search-record line with its set-aside reason (§3); missing evidence is not FAIL.
- Freeze the discovery output after both bounded routes and source/card review are complete. Do not expand a stream into several hours of open-ended searching because a model offers more ideas.

```text
Mode: [CONSOLIDATION / CLEAN DISCOVERY]

                          shared-master edits, and Claude outputs/]

Freeze: WORKING [replace with FROZEN and Almaty timestamp]
```

Under `## Hypotheses`, use one fenced CSV block with exactly this header. It is the common exchange format; it does not replace the existing longlist/evidence templates used later.

```csv
id,opportunity_name,investor_type,capital_decision,buyer_reachable_market,trigger_why_now,kz_location_asset,strongest_alternative,plausible_kz_deciding_advantage,why_still_open,cheapest_falsification_test,initial_evidence_links,important_unknowns
```

Use one record per hypothesis. Quote CSV cells containing commas or quotation marks; double quotation marks inside quoted cells. Keep each record on one physical line. Separate multiple evidence links with ` ; ` and include useful dates/locators. This lets the merge owner extract the same CSV block from all four files without interpreting different layouts. Do not add a fabricated example row. Zero hypotheses means a header-only block plus the route-completion note.

Assign `GZ-001`, `AB-001`, `SG-001` or `BK-001` onward within the correct stream. IDs are stable once issued: do not reuse a removed/merged ID. Nobody issues final `OP-###` IDs during independent work.

**Consolidation outputs only:** add `## Provenance` with a separate fenced CSV block:

```csv
id,original_source_provenance,aliases_duplicate_mappings
```

This is the minimal mapping needed to recover where a record came from: working ID → original source file / AI run and original record ID or locator (`original_source_provenance`) → any absorbed working IDs or aliases (`aliases_duplicate_mappings`). Use one row per original occurrence; repeat the retained working ID when several originals map to it. Missing provenance remains `not established`. Do not carry stars, rankings, Top-5 labels or promotional wording into the provenance block or the hypothesis CSV; they remain only in the untouched historical source files. A block already written with the earlier extra ranking column is acceptable; the merge ignores that column. Clean outputs need no provenance block; their external evidence remains in the common hypothesis fields.

**Clean-discovery outputs only:** alongside the hypothesis block, keep a lightweight search record in the same owned file — normally about 5–10 meaningful entries, one line each: `route | search direction / source examined | useful evidence (link) or nothing useful | outcome`. The outcome is either the submitted working ID or, for a concrete lead that was not submitted, a set-aside reason: `time`, `access`, `cap`, `evidence against` or `too vague / no capital decision`. Record meaningful directions, not clicks. Set-aside leads stay one line each; they are not cards, and the record is not another longlist. End the record with 1–2 lines on any material coverage, access or language limitation and why searching stopped. Add the three-line falsification note for each submitted card (§2). None of this adds columns to the common hypothesis schema.

### Reconvene coverage check (one bounded safeguard)

The merged pool will likely contain many more consolidated than clean-discovery records. Neutral screening removes author bias but cannot recover a lead that never entered the universe. During the normal reconvene discussion, after initial screening statuses exist, ask once:

> Are the serious survivors overly dependent on the same underlying mechanism or assumption, while a concrete, externally anchored but underdeveloped clean-search lead was omitted mainly because of time, access or the card cap?

1. Look at the PASS / consequential UWT survivor set and ask whether it is concentrated around substantially the same mechanism or assumption.
2. Sagadat and Beknur may surface up to a few concrete set-aside leads from their existing search records tagged `time`, `access` or `cap`.
3. A lead is relevant only if it has a real external anchor, a materially different investor capital decision / mechanism and a specific test that could plausibly change the finalist pool.
4. Humans may authorize at most **two** targeted recovery tests, each stating what existing work it displaces. Zero recovery tests is completely acceptable.
5. A recovered hypothesis receives a neutral `OP-###` ID and normal screening treatment. Novelty or clean-scout origin gives it no automatic advantage.

No coverage dashboard, sector-balance matrix, scoring, reserved finalist slots, diversity quotas, card-cap increase, new longlist or discovery sprint. Then discovery closes.

### Human decisions and AI support

AI may heavily support search, extraction, normalization, deduplication proposals, source finding, calculations, contradiction searches and drafting. Humans own: final PASS / UWT / PARK / FAIL decisions; uncertain deduplication / merge calls; allocation of substantial validation effort; survivor selection; the final strongest-rival judgment; the final Top 5; teaser selection; the final storyline and headlines; and whether decisive evidence actually supports a claim.
